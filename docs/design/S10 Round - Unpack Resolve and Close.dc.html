F1 — A CONTAINER TAKES AN OUTCOMEDRAWN
Yes. A5 excluded containers from the packing arithmetic because a journey stands in for a status; an outcome is a third register and the duffel has one — it is depot gear that must come home or be lost, and a Depot that cannot say so drifts, which is what this slice exists to stop. So F5's denominator is not F4's: F4 counts what carries a status, F5 counts what comes home. The board's twin 61 was a mock coincidence; the trip that reads 48/61 PIECES on F4 reads 56/62 here (61 − 1 trip-only + 2 depot containers). The container row is an ordinary row: no rail (the rail is F4's control), meta → SHELF L-TOP · 12 INSIDE, and its outcome sheet states ITS CONTENTS KEEP THEIR OWN OUTCOMES. — no cascade, ever. Frame at 01.

F7 — THE PER-PERSON ROW AND ITS SHEETDRAWN
One row per Entry, a 34px cluster, one control — every Piece of one gear shares one return path, and N rows repeating it is the identical-rows fault (B3, I). The circles encode three values, not four: filled = resolved (back or consumed), bordered = open, attention ring = lost — the tone Find's per-person card already draws for exactly this Piece. Consumed is not drawable as a fourth tone at 34px without inventing one, and it differs from back only in the Depot count, which the sheet states; the cluster's count reads resolved over Pieces. The sheet is the outcome sheet with a roster above the verbs: 48px rows, 30px circles, each row a selection (✓), EVERYONE selected on open — so SET EVERYONE is the default rather than a second control — and the four chips apply to the selection, one op per Piece that changes (§5g E10).
