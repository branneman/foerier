K4 — THE WHOLE TAG CHIP IS THE REMOVE TARGET; THE ✕ IS ITS READOUTREVERSED
The 44×40 trade is refused, and so is widening the chip. Both accept the premise that the ✕ is the target and the label is scenery — and the app has already ruled the other way three times: the whole row is the tap target (list anatomy), the whole row ticks and the square is the readout (§5l I22), the cluster and its count are one control (§5d B). So: in the picker's ON THIS GEAR row and on gear detail's chips, the chip is one control that removes, and the ✕ is the mark that says so. The target is then the chip's whole painted box — 32 tall by its own label's width, comfortably past 44 wide — the ::after disappears, the 40 disappears with it, and a tap on the word does what the word's own ✕ announces instead of nothing. Removal already confirms nothing (§4: one op, instantly reversible), which is what makes a whole-chip target safe. Accessible name Remove #cooking.

K13 — ACTION FIRST, CANCEL AFTER — IN THE CARD REGISTER TOOCHANGED
The S3-era cards draw Cancel-first and the S9 card draws Action-first, and the code follows whichever board it was built against — a real finding, and the sheet register settled it years of slices ago. K14 — THE SWEEP: TWO COUNT GRAMMARS, AND EACH ANSWERS ITS OWN QUESTIONBLESSED
Six count strings measured, and the apparent inconsistency resolves into two grammars with a stated test. N OF M narrows a set — a filter's read, 9 OF 128, 23 OF 128. N/M measures progress through one — 48/61, 3/7, 56/62. And the word after the fraction: PIECES names the unit, RESOLVED and TICKED name the state. The rule that makes both right: where the fraction measures distance to a gate, the word is the state; where there is no gate, the word is the unit. F5's numerator was ruled resolved precisely because its bar measures the distance to invariant 18's gate (F2); F4's packing has no gate, so its own fraction is free to name what it counts, and its companion segment (13 LEFT) carries the state. A bare N NOTES is neither — a count with no denominator states a size, and I3 already ruled its inflection.

K16 — SIDE, ALIGNMENT, WIDTH AND COLLISIONDRAWN
Side: bottom, always. Never right — five of the seven triggers are a row's right-edge control, which at Split sits a gutter from the pane edge, so a side popover collides on open every time. Alignment follows the trigger's own edge, which is one rule and not seven: end for a right-edge trigger in a row (cluster, pill), start for a left-aligned one in a form or a bar (ghost chip, picked-value row). Width min(20rem, available), floored at the trigger's own width — 20rem is the phone sheet's content measure the seven were authored at, and available is Radix's own collision-aware value, so the cap is a rule rather than a number about a pane. Collision: avoid on, flip bottom→top allowed, shift along the align axis allowed, padding = the shell gutter. The content scrolls itself at max-height: available — the sheets' own svh discipline, one axis over — so a short viewport never produces a clipped popover.
K17 — THE TRIGGER STAYS THE CALLER'S; THE ANCHOR IS THE SEAMDRAWN
Radix wants trigger and content as siblings under one root; the app's convention is mounted is open (§4.1), and mount is what resets a picker's drafts. Both survive, and the resolution is that Popover.Trigger is not used at all. The root wraps an anchor and the content, open is driven by the caller's own state, and the content is still written {open && …} — so drafts still reset on mount, and the caller's existing button keeps its own props, its accessible name and its hit extension, none of which a Radix trigger would leave alone. ui/Popover therefore takes anchor as a ReactNode, which is GearRow's anchorProps idiom and the same shape Sheet and Confirm use to keep routing in app/. Nothing about the seven callers' triggers is redesigned; each gains one wrapper and loses its desktopCard.

K28 — FOUR STEPS AT THE SMALL END, NAMED FOR THE BAND THEY LABELCHANGED
A Foundations change, and the reason 89 hand-edits were never attempted. Five raw sizes ship today (11 · 10 · 9.5 · 9 · 8.5, plus a 10.5 on the tag chip) and they are not five decisions — they are four jobs: the label itself, a label riding a control, a label in chrome or a hint, a column head or eyebrow. So four steps, and every value derived from the one drawn pair rather than chosen: --text-label 11/14 (drawn) · --text-label-control 10/13 · --text-label-chrome 9/12 · --text-label-head 8.5/11.5. Line-height is size + 3, which is what the drawn 11/14 states and what a caps run with no descenders wants. Tracking rises as size falls across the drawn .05–.14em range, one value per step: .08 · .10 · .12 · .14. The recipe becomes a utility class, .label and three modifiers in the utilities layer — which is where a global class is legal, so it needs neither composes nor :global, and a test can assert a class where it could never assert 170 raw rems.

WHY A CLASS AND NOT A TOKEN SET ALONE
The recipe is three declarations, and tokens close only one of them — nineteen modules carry all three verbatim.
