00 — THE REGISTER · J. DELETING A TRIP (J1–J6)
J1 — DELETE LIVES AT THE FOOT OF THE TRIP SCREENDELETEDRAWN
Settled before the round and drawn here: /trips/:id only, every phase, Draft included — a Draft made by mistake is the commonest thing anyone deletes. Attention-coloured mono text DELETE TRIP, the SIGN OUT / REMOVE treatment, never a filled red button; standalone, so drawn ≥48 (§5b O). It is the screen's last element at every width, under a 1px rule, 24 below whatever the list ends with. No ▲ — the glyph is reserved for an act that discards unsynced work, and a tombstone is an ordinary op that syncs like any other.
J2 — THE FACT BLOCK READS THE SCREEN'S OWN ORDERDELETEDRAWN
Confirm's one shape (§5k H3): title → mono fact lines → answer → note → primary + Cancel. Four conditional lines, each absent at zero (G3), in the trip screen's own document order — 7 TASKS · 4 NOTES · 35 ENTRIES · 17 PIECES — then 56 OUTCOMES · 1 LOST last, because outcomes are the one register the trip screen does not carry (they are F5's). A Quartermaster reads the block against the screen they are standing on. N NOTES counts discarded ones (I16). On an empty Draft there is no block — J3's answer is the same sentence at every size of Trip, and a fabricated 0 ENTRIES would be the first zero segment written since G3.
J3 — THE ANSWER LINE, AND WHAT IS NOT TOUCHEDDELETEDRAWN
Answer, default ink, said once: Permanent. No route puts a trip back. Muted explainer note beneath it: The depot is untouched. An entry lists gear; it never holds it. Two registers, not one paragraph (H9). No invented restore, no "Are you sure?", no exclamation. Primary takes §12's bordered-attention 48 Delete trip + ghost Cancel — the reopen confirm's accent primary is for a confirm that throws nothing away, and this one does. A decision, so the scrim does not dismiss; Escape does.
J4 — AFTER THE TAP: /trips, AND NO UNDODELETERULED
The sheet closes and the screen it stood on is gone, so the app lands on /trips with the Trip already absent — one selector filters the tombstone, so the list, whereabouts, claims, the TRIP dimension and the nav count are all already right. No toast. And no UNDO, against the standing "UNDO appears after every change": no op restores a Trip, so an UNDO would offer a route that does not exist. The undo of a delete is the Cancel, taken before instead of after — which is the whole reason invariant 15 asks for a confirm. Named as conflict 5.
J5 — A PEER'S DELETE: AN EMPTY STATE, NEVER A REDIRECTDELETEDRAWN
A screen that teleports reads as a defect, so /trips/:id for a Trip the fold cannot show renders a ledger-voice empty state and stays put. The fold can tell the two cases apart, so the screen says which: a tombstone gives TRIP DELETED + It was deleted on this or another device. The depot is untouched.; an id that has not folded gives TRIP NOT ON THIS DEVICE + It may not have synced here yet. This clears itself. — §5b F's rule, a sync race is not an error: no amber, no ▲, self-clearing. Bordered 48 Open trips; the band's back link is the second route and follows §5b's own gate (absent at Desktop, where the sidebar carries TRIPS).
J6 — NO SECOND DOORDELETEDECLINED
Asked for the closed ledger row, and refused. The row already carries a name, a date, two or three counts, REOPEN and a ›; a destructive control at the end of it is exactly the accident invariant 15 guards against, and it would sit one thumb-width from the one control that restores a Trip. Delete is worth a screen. The active and dashed cards carry nothing either: they are the doors to a Trip, not to its removal.

10 — CONFLICTS, NAMED
1 · THE SUGGESTION BAND vs SYNC-PROTOCOL §4.5
Resolved, in the protocol's favour. Screens B's 1024 frame is redrawn without the band; its three strings retire; S7's spec sentence handing the band to S14 as from_trip_id's reader is honoured by J18's header line rather than by the band. The protocol's determinism argument is the later and better one and it is load-bearing; the band predates it.
2 · CLEARED AT CLOSE vs A COPY THAT OUTLIVES THE CLOSE
Resolved, no string moves. The phrase is about this Entry on this Trip and stays true: the copy is a new Entry, minted by the batch, on a Trip that has not closed. The reading that makes it false treats the name as an identity, which is the one thing a trip-only Entry does not have (invariant 9 forbids two Trips referencing one, not two Trips each holding their own). Recorded so the next round does not rediscover it as a defect.
3 · STORY 14'S ENUMERATION vs THE INERT ROWS IT PRODUCES
Open, and left open deliberately. Participants are absent from story 14's list and from §4.5's batch, so they do not copy, and a hüttentour list started from a template lands with every per-person row inert. The round draws that first impression, accepts it, and discloses it before the create (J10's second line) rather than explaining it after. It does not overturn §2: the exclusion is an enumeration rather than a reasoned rule, so overturning it is a story change through the requirements process, and a frame is not the place to make one. Recommendation to the maintainer: if the household hits this in use, the question to put to requirements is whether story 14's list should gain Participants — not whether the design should paper over it.
4 · "A PAST ONE" vs EVERY VISIBLE TRIP
Ruled wider than the words. J8 offers closed, active and Draft Trips alike, on the S7 TRIP-dimension precedent and because copying only reads the source. Story 14's a past one is read as the motivating case, not a fence. Flagged rather than resolved silently: if requirements meant it as a constraint, the picker's list is a one-line change and the meta line, sort and fact copy all stand.
5 · "UNDO APPEARS AFTER EVERY CHANGE" vs A DELETE WITH NO RESTORE OP
Found by this round, ruled here. The standing interaction rule cannot hold for trip.deleted: no op restores a Trip, so an UNDO would offer a route that does not exist — the exact failure the withdrawal rule exists to prevent. The rule gains its stated exception: a confirmed destructive act carries no UNDO, because its undo is the Cancel, taken before instead of after. This is also why invariant 15 asks for a confirm at all, so the two rules agree once the exception is written down.
